QUESTION:
What factors might be associated with persistent school absence?

----------------------------------------------------------------------

RESEARCH BRIEF:

## Problem
Persistent school absence is a significant concern for local authorities, as it can have long-term effects on a child's educational attainment and overall well-being. Students who miss regular days of school may struggle to catch up on missed learning, which can lead to lower academic achievement and higher dropout rates.

## Research Questions
1. What factors are associated with persistent school absence in children aged 5-16?
2. Do socio-economic status and family background influence the likelihood of persistent school absence?
3. How does the impact of persistent school absence on attendance vary by year group (e.g., primary, secondary)?

## Population or Groups
The population of interest includes all children in local schools who have missed three or more consecutive days of school within a given period (e.g., term or academic year). The groups may include:

* Children from disadvantaged socio-economic backgrounds
* Children with special educational needs
* Children living in areas with high levels of poverty or crime

## Potentially useful data or variables
Potential data sources and variables that might be used to investigate this topic include:

* National child health and wellbeing surveys (e.g., NCHW)
* Schools' attendance records and catch-up data
* Socio-demographic information (e.g., census data, school admissions data)
* Health and wellbeing data (e.g., medical history, mental health records)
* School demographics (e.g., class size, staffing levels)

## Suggestions
To address this research question, analytical methods such as:

* Multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) to examine the relationships between socio-economic status and family background with persistent school absence
* Logistic regression analysis to identify factors associated with persistent school absence, controlling for other variables such as age and sex

## Explain how the findings may support a decision.
The findings from this research could inform local authority decisions on:

* Developing targeted interventions for families from disadvantaged backgrounds or those with children with special educational needs
* Improving attendance tracking and monitoring systems in schools to reduce persistent absences
* Investing in school-based programs that address underlying factors contributing to persistent school absence (e.g., mental health support, family engagement initiatives)

## State assumptions and missing information
Assumptions:

* The relationship between socio-economic status and family background with persistent school absence is bidirectional (i.e., both cause and effect).
* Schools have adequate resources to track attendance and monitor student progress.

Missing information:

* Data on the specific causes of persistent school absence (e.g., mental health issues, family conflicts)
