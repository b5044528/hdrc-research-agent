QUESTION:
What factors might be associated with persistent school absence?

----------------------------------------------------------------------

RESEARCH BRIEF:

## Problem
Persistent school absence is a significant concern in many educational settings, leading to potential gaps in education and social isolation for affected children. It can also have long-term effects on their academic performance, mental health, and overall well-being.

## Research Questions
1. What are the most common reasons for persistent school absence among children in our local authority?
2. Is there a correlation between persistent school absence and socio-economic status, family dynamics, or other potential risk factors?
3. How do parents' perceptions of their child's attendance impact their own behavior and decisions regarding attendance?

## Population or Groups
The population of interest includes:
- Children aged 5-16 attending schools in our local authority.
- Parents or guardians of these children.
- School staff and administrators.

## Potentially useful data or variables
- Attendance records for individual students over a specified period (e.g., 12 months).
- Socio-economic status, as measured by family income, parental education level, and occupation.
- Demographic information about the student population, including age, sex, ethnicity, and special educational needs.
- Data on the number of holidays taken and absences due to medical reasons.
- Parental surveys or questionnaires assessing their attitudes towards attendance, child behavior, and decision-making processes.

## Suggestions
- Descriptive statistics (e.g., frequency tables) to identify common reasons for persistent school absence.
- Bivariate analysis (e.g., correlation coefficient) between socio-economic status and attendance rates.
- Multivariate analysis (e.g., logistic regression) to control for potential confounding variables in the relationship between socio-economic status and persistent school absence.

## Explain how the findings may support a decision
Understanding the factors associated with persistent school absence can inform targeted interventions aimed at reducing absenteeism. Data on common reasons for absence can help schools develop more effective policies for addressing truancy. Identifying correlations between socio-economic status and attendance rates can lead to policy changes that address inequality in access to education.

## State assumptions and missing information
Assumptions:
- The data collected is representative of the entire student population.
- Parents' perceptions of their child's attendance are accurate and not influenced by biases.

Missing information:
- A clear understanding of how changes in socio-economic status affect attendance rates over time.
- Data on the effectiveness of existing interventions aimed at reducing absenteeism.
